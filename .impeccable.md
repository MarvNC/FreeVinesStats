## Design Context

### Users
Amazon Vine power users and frequent deal hunters who check drop patterns obsessively throughout the day. They understand Vine jargon (0 ETV, AFA, AI) and want dense, actionable data at a glance. Often checking on mobile between drops. Emotional state: anticipatory, slightly anxious about missing good items, but also enjoying the hunt.

### Brand Personality
- **Voice:** Insider-friendly, playful, warm
- **Tone:** Like a cozy tool built by a fellow Vine user — thoughtful, human, premium
- **3 words:** Warm, sophisticated, delightful
- **Emotional goals:** Make obsessive data checking feel like a pleasant ritual, not a clinical chore

### Aesthetic Direction: "Midnight Botanical"
A warm, organic, sophisticated aesthetic that feels like a premium data experience. Think: a botanist's field journal meets a cozy late-night command center. Rich with information but wrapped in warmth and intentionality.

**Visual Characteristics:**
- **Warm neutrals:** Shift from clinical cool grays (slate) to warm, inviting tones (zinc, stone, warm charcoal)
- **Organic accents:** Muted gold, soft ochre, warm terracotta as primary accent — never neon or electric
- **Sophisticated data colors:** Desaturated, harmonious chart colors that feel natural and premium:
  - Sage/forest green for primary data series
  - Soft burgundy/rosewood for secondary
  - Warm ochre/mustard for tertiary
- **OKLCH heatmaps:** Keep the blue-to-magenta scale but ensure it harmonizes with the warm palette
- **Typography:** Outfit for headlines (geometric, friendly), Plus Jakarta Sans for body. No serif fonts. Sentence case labels, not aggressive uppercase.
- **No card grids:** Avoid the generic "white card on gray background" dashboard pattern
- **Subtle depth:** Use 1px borders and background color shifts for separation, not shadows or rounded cards
- **Human spacing:** Generous but intentional whitespace. Not cramped, not wasteful.

**Anti-references:**
- Neon/cyberpunk aesthetics (too harsh, causes eye strain)
- Generic SaaS dashboards (white cards, drop shadows, rounded corners)
- Corporate clinical tools (cool grays, sterile metrics)
- Editorial magazine layouts (serif fonts, asymmetric editorial compositions)
- Terminal/hacker aesthetics (monospace overload, scanlines, gimmicky effects)

### Theme Behavior
- **Light mode:** Warm off-white background (`#fafaf9`), like cream-colored paper. Feels bright but soft — morning sunlight through a window.
- **Dark mode:** Deep warm charcoal (`#1c1917`), like a cozy study at night. Not pitch black — warm, inviting, easy on the eyes during obsessive late-night checking.

### Design Principles
1. **Warmth over coolness:** Every color choice should feel inviting, not clinical. Even error states should feel human.
2. **Lovely density:** Information-rich but wrapped in thoughtful design. Like a well-designed book — dense but pleasant to read.
3. **Anti-template:** Avoid predictable patterns: logo-left-controls-right headers, three equal stat cards, card-grid layouts, gradient accent lines.
4. **Organic data viz:** Chart colors should feel like they came from nature — moss, clay, bark — not a neon sign.
5. **Human typography:** Sentence case, comfortable reading sizes, no aggressive uppercase tracking. Labels should whisper, not shout.
6. **Intentional hierarchy:** Use size, weight, and spacing to create hierarchy. Not boxes inside boxes.
7. **No gimmicks:** No scanlines, no blinking cursors, no animated gradients, no glassmorphism. The personality comes from color, typography, and thoughtful details.

### Interaction Patterns
- **Hover states:** Subtle background color shifts, gentle opacity changes. No dramatic transforms.
- **Focus states:** Clean 2px outline in primary accent color. Visible but not jarring.
- **Transitions:** Smooth 150-200ms ease-out. Never bouncy or elastic.
- **Data updates:** Gentle pulse or fade on changing numbers. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Header Pattern
- NO gradient accent lines
- NO boxed icons
- NO two-tone split logos
- Should feel like a brand mark, not a SaaS nav bar
- Consider: icon + title lockup inline, or title with subtle tagline
- Controls integrated naturally, not like a separate toolbar

### Card Pattern
- AVOID card wrappers around sections
- Use full-bleed background color zones for separation
- If cards are absolutely necessary, use minimal styling: thin border, no shadow, slight background tint
- Never nest cards inside cards

### Filter / Control Pattern
- Controls should float cleanly on the background
- NO wrapper containers with borders/backgrounds around button groups
- Segmented controls should feel native to the surface, not like embedded widgets
